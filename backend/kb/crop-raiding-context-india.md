# Crop raiding in India

## Scale of losses

A study by the Gokhale Institute estimated that Maharashtra alone loses ₹10,000 to ₹40,000 crore a year to crop damage by wild animals.

## Compensation gap

The same study found that even when about 25% of affected farmers sought compensation, only 1 to 2% received payments that matched the damage they suffered. Delays, paperwork and lack of proof are common reasons claims fail or are underpaid.

## Verification with drones

Reports on the new crop insurance cover note that losses will be verified using drones before compensation is paid. Clear evidence of the animal visit, recorded at the time, helps when the damage is assessed later.

## Why evidence matters

Because insurance and compensation systems need proof within short deadlines, timestamped footage of the animal, photographs of the damage, and a record of when the incident happened make a claim stronger and faster to file.

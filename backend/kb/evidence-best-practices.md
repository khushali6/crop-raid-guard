# Evidence best practices for wildlife crop damage

## Record the animal visit

Keep the original camera video. Do not edit, trim or re-save it with another app, because the original file and its timestamp are the strongest proof. Make sure the camera's date and time are set correctly; if the camera clock is wrong, note the correct time when you upload.

## Photograph the damage

Take geotagged photographs of the damaged crop with location turned on in your phone camera. Take one wide photo showing the whole affected area and close photos showing bite marks, trampling or uprooted plants. Photograph the same day if possible.

## Report on time

For PMFBY wild animal cover, report within 72 hours of the incident through the Crop Insurance App or the insurer's helpline. Do not wait for a full assessment before making the first report.

## Keep a tamper-evident record

An evidence pack that lists each clip and photo with its SHA-256 fingerprint and a digital signature lets an officer check that files were not changed after the pack was made. The pack supports your claim but does not guarantee payment and does not replace the official report.

## Protect privacy

If people appear in the footage, share only clips or frames where they are not identifiable, or blur them before sharing.

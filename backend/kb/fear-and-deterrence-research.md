# Fear and crop raiding

## Research finding

A study reported by Mongabay India in March 2026 suggests that fear of humans can deter wild animals from damaging crops. Animals that perceive a risk of encountering people tend to spend less time feeding in fields.

## Related view

At the AAD 2026 conference, IISER scientist Milind Watve argued that a complete hunting ban has reduced animals' fear of humans, which he linked to increased crop losses (reported by Down To Earth). This is a debated view; any change to wildlife law is a matter for government, and farmers must follow the Wildlife (Protection) Act.

## Practical meaning for farmers

Measures that signal human presence at the times animals visit, such as night watch, lights, sound or people moving along the field edge when an alert comes in, may reduce damage. Knowing the exact hours when animals usually arrive (for example from camera alerts) helps farmers schedule watch and ward at the right time instead of all night.
